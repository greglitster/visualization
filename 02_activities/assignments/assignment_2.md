## Assignment 2: Good and Bad Data Visualization

### Requirements:

- Data visualizations are important tools for communication and convincing; we need to be able to evaluate the ways that data are presented in visual form to be critical consumers of information 
- To test your evaluation skills, locate two public data visualizations online, one good and one bad  
    - You can find data visualizations at https://public.tableau.com/app/discover or https://datavizproject.com/, or anywhere else you like! 
- For each visualization (good and bad):  
    - Explain (with reference to material covered up to date, along with readings and other scholarly sources, as needed) why you classified that visualization the way you did.
      
      #### Good visualization
      ```
      This visualization (https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/jenny.martin/viz/GBPUSDExchangeRate/GBP-USD) from Jenny Martin in the UK shows the historical GBP to USD exchange rate from 1972-2020. This is a good visualization because it has proper labels in all of the necessary spots (e.g., main title, y-axis, x-axis). It shows a lot of information for each year including the highest and lowest exchange rate as well as what it was at the beginning and end of the year. This is helpful to demonstrate major changes in the exchange right like in 2008. The time series plot is also really useful to show the overall decreasing trend of the exchange rate over time, demonstrating that the GBP is closer in value now to the USD. 
      ```
    - How could this data visualization have been improved?  
      ```
      This data visualization could be improve in a couple of ways. The first is to provide a small legend that shows the reasoning for the red and blue values. Though the reader can interpret that blue is a gain in that year and red is a loss, a small indicator would guarantee that the reader knows what the mean is. It could also include an overall trend line to show that the value decreased over that time period. I would also change the values for the years at the bottom to start and end at the first and last data points so that they line up with axis better. 
      ```
      #### Bad visualization
      ```
        The visualization Figure A on page 3 of this report https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/research/report/2026-betting-on-boys-report_final-for-web.pdf demonstrates the percentage of boys age 11 to 17 who gambled in the past month. The visualization is bad because it uses a trend line between datapoints that is unnecessary, there are errors where the datapoints actually are in relation to the axis and it is missing some labels on the axis as well. They also make a claim in the subtitle of the figure that is not shown at all in the visualization. The errors in the percentage reporting (e.g., 32% data being at the level of 20% on the axis) makes the graph innaccurate.  
      ```
      - How could this data visualization been improved?
      ```
        This data visualization should have been a bar chart showing the percentage of their survery respondents who answered that they had gambled in the last 12 months, removing the trend line between the data points as it does not add any value to the chart currently. Each of the bars would represent a specific age and would need to be the same width. They could then also remove the individual percentages to remove clutter, the data could be read from the y-axis. It would also help to have the y-axis go from 0%-100% instead of stopping at 60%. The data points need to also be accurate and placed at the correct level on the y-axis. The claim that they make in the subtitle could also be shown with an asterisk or separate colour to show that the differences between the other age groups are statistically significant. The y-axis title needs to be added and might read as 'percentage of boys who gambled in the past twelve months'. 
      ```
