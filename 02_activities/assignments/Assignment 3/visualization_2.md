## Visualization 2
This visualization was created using the dataset from the Ontario Research Funding Summary webpage found here (https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/ontario-research-funding-summary-current/resource/ebf11cfd-37f7-4775-b2d7-de4f1343787a). The data set provides a summary of research projects funded by the Ministry of Colleges and Universities in the Province of Ontario. The Excel spread sheet is also included in this repository. 


### What software did you use to create your data visualization?
I used Microsoft excel to create this visualization

### Who is your intended audience? 
The intended audience for this is members of the Ontario public that are interested in how the province of Ontario is spending tax dollars to fund specific areas of research. 
    
### What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 
I am trying to demonstrate the trend and differences in funding for research depending on the discipline they are in. This reveals the priorites of the government, showing that it has a higher interest in funding projects related to engineering and technology, medical, health and life sciences as well as the natural sciences compared to social sciences and humanities. 
    
### What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots? 
I considered the colours of the trend lines and ensuring they are accessible and differentiable. I also considered the size of the fonts for the axes. 
    
### How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 
Anyone with excel or similar tool could have created this visualization with access to the dataset listed above. However, using Microsoft Excel to create this visualization doesn't guarentee that it would reproducible because there is no trail of the decisions I made without access to code. Another person creating this visualization might have selected other discplines or research areas to include depending on their interests, biases, and trends they notice in the data. 
    
### How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?  
I tried to use a blend of colours that most people will be able to see as well as descriptive titles with large fonts for each of the axis labels. 
    
### Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?
Anyone who is interested in getting research funding might be swayed by which disciplines are receiving the most funding over the last 5 or so years. For example, a recent graduate considering which grad school program to apply to might look at this graph and see that STEM disciplines seem to be better supported overall and chose one of those disciplines. 
    
### How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 
This was done through a process of trial and error. A number of missing datapoints for other disciplines or research areas prevented them from being included in the graph. I also selected the disciplines based on scale of the y-axis to show obvious differences between them becuase most other research areas or disciplines recieved similar amounts of funding resulting in a graph with a number of straight lines. 

### What ‘underwater labour’ contributed to your final data visualization product?
Selecting and organizing the pivot table used to create the data was underwater labour. All of the decisions that I made along the way are also not documented compared to the visualization created using Python.
